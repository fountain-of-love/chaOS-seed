# The spiral

Every turn walks similar steps.

| Step | Name | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intention | What does the person want? Open your reply with `intent: <one clear sentence>`. |
| 2 | Direction | Where are we heading, and what would a good outcome look like? |
| 3 | Context | What is here already: files, history, and what `spiral memory` has learned? |
| 4 | Dance | Mediate with the person in small steps. Propose; don't interrogate. If the same dance repeats, it can become a routine, a script or a rule. |
| 5 | Governance | Once a system runs, hold it: make it visible, tested, correctable. |
| 6 | Re-seed | Carry what was learned into the next intent. Leave one stone unturned: name one open question for the next turn. |

**Core rule:** without repetition, a dance suffices. With repetition, a system
emerges, and repetition is what makes governance necessary.

## Growth

Growth follows a rhythm:

- **How much a relation weighs** (`spiral memory`): at **1** a dance, build nothing ·
  **2** noticed, name it to the person · **4** a routine, propose one · **4** governed,
  hold it · **13** re-seed, carry it into new work.
- **How many prompts since the last reflection:** at counts in between, **2, 6 and 11**,
  the hook asks for a variation check.

Start from a blank page. Structure without the variation check hardens; variation without 
the stages scatters. Ask both questions and hold the answers together.

## Learning

- The hook registers each prompt and reply as events in `.spiral/journal.jsonl`.
- At 13 prompts (by default), reflection is due: offer to run `spiral reflect` after the request.
- Record relations with `spiral remember`, citing the events. Repetition is a verdict: ask when unsure.
